About me

Hi, I’m Tracy.
Always learning.

I never had one perfectly straight career path. I had a lot of curiosity, a habit of figuring things out, and a tendency to carry useful lessons from one chapter into the next.

One job taught me how to talk to people. Another taught me how to troubleshoot. Art taught me composition. Teaching taught me patience. Software made me curious about how systems are built. Freelancing eventually put all of those pieces in the same room.

Where it started

I started by saying yes to things I wanted to understand.

The story behind the range

I did not have to leave the old skills behind to learn how to use the new ones.

The different chapters became useful together. Customer-facing work made me better at communication. Technical support made me more patient with problems. Art made me notice detail. Teaching made me think about how information lands. Digital work gave all of those habits somewhere new to go.

People

I learned how to listen, explain, build rapport and stay useful even when the request is unclear at first.

Creative work

I learned how to use composition, color, pacing, framing and visual hierarchy to make an idea easier to feel and understand.

Systems

I learned to look at what happens after the asset is finished: where it goes, who needs it next, how it gets tracked and how the process becomes easier to repeat.

Food + culinary arts

My hobbies are usually another excuse to learn with my hands.

Cooking and baking keep me curious about technique, texture, timing and presentation. I like testing why something works, changing one variable at a time, and learning from the result. It is creative, but it is also surprisingly systematic.

Some days that means bread, cookies or plated meals. Other days it is simply setting a table and noticing how presentation changes the experience. The interest stays personal, but the habit of observing and refining follows me back into work.

Fine art + teaching

Art taught me to look longer. Teaching taught me to explain gently.

I spent years making art, exhibiting it, teaching children and helping other people feel less intimidated by a blank page. Painting trained my eye long before I started thinking about brands, content or digital work. Teaching gave me a different lesson: knowing how to do something is not the same as knowing how to explain it.

That lesson still shows up when I give feedback, write instructions, train someone on a workflow or try to make a complicated process easier to understand.

Learning now

I am still actively building the next layer.

Learning did not stop when I found work I liked. I am still studying technology, web development and internal systems because I want to understand more of the structure behind the tools I already use every day.

Formal learning

I keep taking technology and innovation-related courses because I like understanding the tools behind the work, not only the interface in front of me.

Learning to code

Web development gives me a different way to think about structure, hierarchy, logic and how an experience is built from the inside.

Building systems

I am also learning how to build internal systems because I want workflows to become clearer, easier to hand off and easier to improve.

Music

Not every interest needs to become a job.

Music is one of the places where I can practice without needing to turn it into an output for someone else. I like having interests that remind me to stay a beginner sometimes.

Learning through repetition

Playing gives me another way to practice patience, rhythm and attention without rushing toward a professional result.

Keeping something for myself

I like having a creative practice that is allowed to stay personal and imperfect.

Every piece of knowledge becomes something I can carry forward.
