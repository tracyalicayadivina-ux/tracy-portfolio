Experience

Different industries gave me different ways to understand the work.

My path moved through customer service, sales, technical support, software and creative execution before landing in creative operations.

I do not look at those chapters as random. Each one taught me something I still use now: how to communicate, how to troubleshoot, how to explain value, how to understand a user, how to work inside a system, and how to follow an idea all the way through to a finished output.

The thread

“The thread was never the job title. The thread was learning how things work.”

Career chapters

The useful part kept staying with me.

The roles changed, but the lessons stacked. This is the simpler story behind the formal CV.

Retail + service

Learning how people ask for help

Early customer service work included order taking, multitasking, product questions and handling people who needed a quick, clear answer.

I learned to listen for what someone was actually asking, keep several details in my head at once, and stay useful even when the conversation moved fast.

Finance + tax

Explaining value and building trust

Financial sales work introduced me to tax-related products, upselling and conversations where people needed to understand what they were paying for.

It sharpened the part of me that thinks about positioning, clarity and how trust affects whether someone listens to a recommendation.

Premium food retail

Product presentation became personal

Working around a premium food brand made me more curious about food, product quality and presentation.

That interest followed me outside work into food photography, cooking and baking, which later became another way I learned composition and visual storytelling.

Health technology

Troubleshooting became a real skill

As a Tier 2 technical support specialist for a smart wearable and fitness app, I worked across technical navigation, backend tasks, customer email, health data, sleep information and issue resolution.

I learned to diagnose before reacting, use macros without sounding robotic, and explain technical steps in a way that still felt human. I was recognized as “Rapport Queen” and maintained zero QA callouts during that period.

B2B software

I started looking at the system behind the service

Supporting established business clients inside a software company exposed me to B2B structure, digital products and the way teams depend on systems to keep work organized.

That chapter strengthened my interest in coding, internal tools and how a workflow is designed behind the interface.

Creative operations

The old skills finally met each other

Freelancing and long-term work with a growing fitness brand brought communication, visual work, technical comfort, systems thinking and day-to-day execution into the same role.

Now I can move from a creative brief to an edit, from an edit to the app, from the app to social, and from the launch back into the workflow that prepares the next one.

What that adds up to

I bring more than one lens into the same problem.

The value of having a mixed background is not that I can list more job titles. It is that I can look at a task from the customer side, the creative side, the technical side and the operational side before deciding what needs to happen next.

Communication

Writing, explaining, giving feedback, building rapport and making handoffs easier to understand.

Troubleshooting

Breaking a problem down, finding the actual issue and staying calm while the answer is still unclear.

Creative judgment

Using visual hierarchy, pacing, framing and brand context to shape how an output feels.

Digital confidence

Learning platforms quickly, navigating backends and becoming comfortable with unfamiliar tools.

Operational follow-through

Thinking past the asset into the upload, schedule, review, handoff and next repeatable step.

Learning agility

Turning what I learn in one environment into something useful in the next instead of starting from zero every time.
