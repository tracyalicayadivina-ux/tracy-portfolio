The Short Version

A multidisciplinary mind with a habit of building.

My work has never fit neatly into one title. I have moved through creative work, teaching, customer-facing work, design, content, social media, digital support, and operations.

Instead of seeing those experiences as separate, I see them as layers. Each one taught me something about people, communication, creativity, organization, problem-solving, and execution.

Today, I bring those layers together to help ideas become clearer, work become easier to manage, and growing teams have the structure they need behind the scenes.

Different experiences. One evolving practice.

How I Work

Sometimes the whole team is one person.

I am comfortable stepping into a project, understanding what needs to happen, figuring out what I need to learn, and moving the work forward. I do not need every part of the process to be handed to me first.

If something needs a system, I look for the system. If something needs structure, I build the structure. If something needs research, I investigate. If something needs creative execution, I make it. If someone else needs to take over later, I think about how they will understand what was built.

Self-sufficient does not mean working alone forever.

My Approach

Where creativity meets execution.

A task rarely exists by itself. I like understanding the bigger picture, the people involved, the goals, the challenges, and the opportunities. From there, I build around what matters instead of forcing every problem into the same process.

My multidisciplinary background helps me notice connections between areas which are often treated separately. A content problem might have an operations solution. A branding problem might reveal a messaging problem. A workflow problem might really be a communication problem.

What Makes My Approach Different

I don't start with a template. I start with the situation.

Every company has different people, different strengths, different bottlenecks, different tools and different ways of working. I do not believe every business needs the same workflow.

I prefer to understand what already exists, identify what is working, notice what is creating friction, and build from there. The goal is not to make a business dependent on one person's memory. The goal is to make good work easier to repeat, teach and improve.

What I Want To Leave Behind

Build it. Prove it. Teach it.

I do not want the value of a system to disappear when the person who built it steps away.

I want to build systems, workflows and ways of working which become self-sufficient, proven through use, and refined over time.

Then I want to teach them. Not so the work stays dependent on me, but so other people learn from it, improve it and take it further.
